Step 1: what the panel says

The panel says six things. Each one is a line. For each line you choose whether to show it, and how far away someone can read it from. Reading from further away needs bigger letters, and bigger letters take more space. The panel does not grow, so the lines share it.

How we work out the letter size

There is one rule. Take the reading distance in millimetres and divide it by 100. That is how tall the capital letters need to be, in millimetres.

So a line read from 1 m away (1,000 mm) needs capitals 10 mm tall. A line read from across a corridor (1,400 mm) needs capitals 14 mm tall.

This matches the University's guidance for these panels. It is a generous rule, chosen on purpose so that people with mild sight loss are included.

Step 2: the colours

Each status gets its own colour. Every colour offered below has already passed two tests: the status word reads clearly on it, and it stands out against the panel's dark blue background with no extra border. That leaves … of the University's 45 brand colours.

Two colours count as clearly different when they are at least 20 delta E apart - the bar this programme uses for a pair of statuses. The page measures every pair in normal vision, in three kinds of colour blindness, and in greyscale.

How this page checks its own sums

This page marks its own homework. When it loads, it adds up the space your lines need, then measures the real panel drawing, and the two must agree to within one pixel. It builds a panel that is deliberately too full, and checks that the overflow gets caught. It also re-measures both ready-made colour schemes and compares every figure with the ones this programme recorded when the schemes were chosen - two separate instruments that must agree.
